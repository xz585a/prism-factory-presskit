概要

『Prism Factory』は、白い光を注文どおりの色と明るさに加工して届ける工場ゲームです。

「黄色い光を、強度5で」。注文はその形でやってきます。盤面にあるのは光源1つと、まっすぐ進む白いビームだけ。あなたは盤上に光学装置を置いて、ビームを曲げ、割り、混ぜ、注文どおりの光を届け先へ通します。

装置は、光を増やしません。光源が強度8を出しているなら、そこから盤面へ入る光は8です。2本に分ければ4と4。エネルギー保存則が、そのまま資源の制約になっています。電力も、資金も、部品の在庫もありません。第二の資源を持ち込まないことが、このゲームの設計の中心です。追う数値が1種類しかないので、盤面に見えているものが全部そのまま判断の材料になります。

装置は4種類。これで全部です。ミラーでビームを折り、プリズムで白を赤・緑・青に割り、分光器で1本のビームから必要な分だけを抜き取り、合波器で色を足し合わせる。それでも盤面が難しくなり続けるのは、分光器が抜き取れるのが「半分」「4分の1」「8分の1」だけだからです。3も5も、装置が直接は作れない数です。要求された値をつくるには、割った光をもう一度足し合わせるしかありません。

満たした注文は消えません。ウェーブが進むたびに注文は増え、一度引き受けた注文は以降ずっと供給し続けなければなりません。納品して終わりではなく、通し続ける配線として盤面に残ります。だから盤面は、あなたが引き受けてきたもの全部の記録になります。

増えるのは、光源の本数だけです。数ウェーブに一度、盤面に光源が1本増えます。ただし1本あたりの明るさは最初から最後まで変わりません。手に入るのは強い光ではなく、盤面の別の場所から出てくる、もう1本の白い光です。新しい光源は、そこまで配線を伸ばして既存の網と落ち合わせるまで何の役にも立たない ── 供給が増えるたびに、仕事が増えます。

詰まっているのではなく、見えていないだけです。手が無いように見える盤面のほとんどは、必要な光がすでにどこかで捨てられている盤面です。壁で終わっている支線、要求より多く受け取っている届け先。それを見つけて引き直した瞬間に、盤面が一段軽くなります。どの注文に光が余っていて、どのビームが誰にも使われていないかは常に盤面上に表示されます。隠しません。ただし答えも出しません。

壁の形も光源の位置も色の傾向も違う10枚のマップから選んで始めます。数十ウェーブ後、あなたの工場は端から端まで光が走る一枚の織物になっています。終わりはありません。スコアは、どこまで進めたかです。

特徴

  • 唯一の資源は光 — 電力も資金も在庫もない。分けた分だけ弱くなる
  • 装置は4種類 — ミラー、プリズム、分光器、合波器。覚えるのは5分、悩むのはそのあと
  • 作れない数を作る — 抜き取れるのは 1/2・1/4・1/8 だけ。要求値は割って足し戻す
  • 増えるのは光源の本数 — 強い光ではなく、遠くにもう1本。供給が増えるたびに仕事が増える
  • 注文は消えない — 引き受けた注文は以降ずっと通し続ける。盤面が判断の記録になる
  • 1個で全部が変わる — ビームは直線。装置を1個ずらすと、遠くの注文の色が変わる
  • 余りは必ず盤面にある — 詰まっているのではなく、見えていないだけ
  • 無限セッション — クリアはない。スコアは到達ウェーブ。マップは10枚

About

Prism Factory is a factory game about taking white light and delivering it as the colour and brightness each order demands.

"Yellow light, intensity 5." That is the shape every order arrives in. All the board gives you is one lamp and a white beam travelling in a straight line. You place optical devices to bend it, split it, and blend it until a port gets exactly the light it asked for.

No device creates light. If a lamp emits 8, then 8 is what it puts on the board — no more. Split it in two and you hold 4 and 4. Conservation of energy is the resource constraint. There is no power grid, no money, no parts inventory. Refusing a second resource is the centre of this design. With a single number to track, everything visible on the board is something you can reason with.

Four devices. That is the entire toolbox. A mirror turns a beam. A prism splits white into red, green, and blue. A splitter taps a fraction off a beam and lets the rest carry on. A merger adds colours back together. The board keeps getting harder anyway, because a splitter can only tap a half, a quarter, or an eighth. Three is not a number a device can hand you, and neither is five. To reach the value an order wants, you have to split light and add it back together.

A filled order never goes away. Every wave brings another one, and once you take an order you supply it forever. Nothing is ever delivered and done — it stays on the board as a line you have to keep feeding. Which makes the board a record of everything you have agreed to.

What grows is the number of lamps. Every few waves another lamp appears — but a single lamp is exactly as bright on the last wave as it was on the first. What you are handed is never stronger light; it is another white beam, somewhere else on the board, worth nothing until you run a line out to it and bring it together with what you have already placed. Every time supply grows, you get more work.

You are not stuck. You just cannot see it. Most boards that look hopeless are boards where the light you need is already there and already being thrown away — a tapped line ending at a wall, a port taking more than it asked for. Find it, rewire, and the whole board loosens by a notch. Which orders are getting more light than they need, and which beams nobody is using, is shown on the board at all times. Nothing is hidden. Nothing is solved for you either.

Start on any of ten maps, each with its own walls, lamp placements, and colour profile. Dozens of waves later, your factory is one sheet of light running edge to edge. There is no ending. Your score is how far you got.

Key features

  • Light is the only resource — No power, no money, no inventory. Every split costs what it gives
  • Four devices — Mirror, prism, splitter, merger. Five minutes to learn, hours to put down
  • Numbers no device can make — Taps come in halves, quarters, eighths. Split it and add it back
  • Growth means more lamps — Never brighter light. Every time supply grows, so does the work
  • Orders are forever — Take one and you supply it for the rest of the run
  • One device, whole board — Beams run straight. Nudge one device and a distant order changes colour
  • The slack is always there — You are never stuck. You just cannot see it yet
  • Endless sessions — Nothing to clear. Your score is the wave you reached. Ten maps
  • Drawn, not painted — Every pixel on screen is rendered. No image assets at all

Developer

Nulpoyo. A solo developer based in Japan, making games alongside a full-time office job.

I built this game to find out how far a single, non-renewable resource can carry a factory. Factory games usually manufacture difficulty out of a second resource — power, money, parts. I wanted to put none of them in, and see whether one number, the total amount of light on the board, could hold a whole game together. The prototype said yes, so that is the game.

The moment I most wanted to build is the one where a board you thought was finished turns out to have been throwing away exactly what you needed. To make that moment possible, spare light and losses are shown on the board at all times. Nothing is hidden. Nothing is solved for you either.

Everything on screen except the text is drawn as you watch. There is not a single image asset in the game. Your score is the wave you reached — but the board is the thing you will want to show people.
