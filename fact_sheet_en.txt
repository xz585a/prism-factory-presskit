About
================================================================

Prism Factory is a factory game about taking white light and delivering it as the colour and
brightness each order demands.

"Yellow light, intensity 5." That is the shape every order arrives in. All the board gives you is
one lamp and a white beam travelling in a straight line. You place optical devices to bend it,
split it, and blend it until a port gets exactly the light it asked for.

No device creates light. If a lamp emits 8, then 8 is what it puts on the board — no more. Split
it in two and you hold 4 and 4. Conservation of energy is the resource constraint. There is no
power grid, no money, no parts inventory. Refusing a second resource is the centre of this design.

There are four devices, and that is the entire toolbox. A mirror turns a beam. A prism splits
white into red, green, and blue. A splitter taps a fraction off a beam and lets the rest carry on.
A merger adds colours back together. The board keeps getting harder anyway, because a splitter can
only tap a half, a quarter, or an eighth. Three is not a number a device can hand you, and neither
is five. To reach the value an order wants, you have to split light and add it back together.

A filled order never goes away. Every wave brings another one, and once you take an order you
supply it forever. Nothing is ever delivered and done — it stays on the board as a line you have
to keep feeding. Which makes the board a record of everything you have agreed to.

What grows is the number of lamps. Every few waves another lamp appears, but a single lamp is
exactly as bright on the last wave as it was on the first. What you are handed is never stronger
light — it is another white beam, somewhere else on the board. Every time supply grows, you get
more work.

Most boards that look hopeless are boards where the light you need is already there and already
being thrown away — a tapped line ending at a wall, a port taking more than it asked for. Find it,
rewire, and the whole board loosens by a notch. You are not stuck. You just cannot see it yet.

Start on any of ten maps, each with its own walls, lamp placements, and colour profile. There is
no ending. Your score is how far you got.

================================================================
Key features
================================================================

- Light is the only resource — No power, no money, no inventory. Every split costs what it gives
- Four devices — Mirror, prism, splitter, merger. Five minutes to learn, hours to put down
- Numbers no device can make — Taps come in halves, quarters, eighths. Split it and add it back
- Growth means more lamps — Never brighter light. Every time supply grows, so does the work
- Orders are forever — Take one and you supply it for the rest of the run
- One device, whole board — Beams run straight. Nudge one device and a distant order changes colour
- The slack is always there — You are never stuck. You just cannot see it yet
- Endless sessions — Nothing to clear. Your score is the wave you reached. Ten maps
- Drawn, not painted — Every pixel on screen is rendered. No image assets at all
- Language support — English and Japanese

================================================================
Developer
================================================================

Nulpoyo. A solo developer based in Japan, making games alongside a full-time office job.

I built this game to find out how far a single, non-renewable resource can carry a factory. Factory
games usually manufacture difficulty out of a second resource — power, money, parts. I wanted to
put none of them in, and see whether one number, the total amount of light on the board, could hold
a whole game together. The prototype said yes, so that is the game.

With a single number to track, everything visible on the board is something you can reason with.
When you come up short, there is only ever one question to ask: where is light going to waste?

The moment I most wanted to build is the one where a board you thought was finished turns out to
have been throwing away exactly what you needed. To make that moment possible, spare light and
losses are shown on the board at all times. Nothing is hidden. Nothing is solved for you either.

Everything on screen except the text is drawn as you watch. There is not a single image asset in
the game. Your score is the wave you reached — but the board is the thing you will want to show
people.
